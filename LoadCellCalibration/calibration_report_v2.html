2. The fit

Each dot is the average of 24 s of a run (the first 6 s are dropped while the load settles). All runs with the chain and strap on count, including the chain-only runs as 0 kg of plates. The line is the best straight line through all of them.

Output (V) = 0.08703 + 0.018610 × plates (kg). The 0.0870 V is the chain-and-strap zero. Force in newtons = (volts − zero) × 526.95.

3. How straight is it, and what is the up/down step?

The dots below are the leftover distance from the line. A bend would show as an arch or a bowl. Instead the points split in two groups: runs on the way up sit a little below the line and runs on the way down a little above it.

  • No bend: the curve term is +0.38 ± 0.37 kg at 250 kg. The straight line is all you need.
  • Going down reads 0.29 kg (about 0.11 % of 250 kg) higher than going up, at every load, even at zero load with the chain on (+0.0029 V, +0.16 kg). Allowing for that one step, the scatter falls from 0.15 kg to 0.05 kg.
  • The slope is the same both ways (18.618 going up, 18.611 mV/kg coming down, 0.04 % apart). Only the zero moved, so it is a shift of the zero, not a change in sensitivity.
  • It cannot be said from one loop whether the step is the cell remembering the load (hysteresis), the zero drifting over time, or both. The two repeated zero runs show the zero rising 2.4 mV (+0.13 kg) in 71 minutes, which accounts for about half of it.

5. The 50 Hz hum

With the laptop charging, the hum is between 1.0 and 3.5 mV in every run, which is below 0.2 kg. With the laptop on battery it was 85 mV (50 kg, 15:14), 58 mV (75 kg, 15:22). The averages of those runs fall in line with all the others, so the hum does not bias a 24 s average. It only matters for live readouts and for peaks, where the app's 100 ms smoothing removes it. Keep the laptop charging from the isolated outlet.

6. Should yesterday's measurements be added?

No, it would add noise more than information. In numbers:

  • Yesterday's range is only a quarter of today's. It carries 3.6 % of the information about the slope. Pooling moves the slope by -0.010 %, which is -0.02 kg at 250 kg.
  • The slopes differ by +0.26 % ± 0.12 % (today higher). That is small but about twice its own uncertainty, a sign that yesterday had extra scatter: a different rig, a warming-up amplifier and 100 mV of hum. Using yesterday's slope alone at 250 kg would read about 0.7 kg low.
  • Different zero. Yesterday's rack rig had a different zero and different hardware, so it needs its own intercept and cannot share one with today's data.
  • Useful as a check. Two different sessions, rigs and hum levels giving the same sensitivity to 0.26 % shows the amplifier gain is stable. If the plates were the same both days, this does not check the plate masses.

7. What to put in the app, and what is left

Calibration to use: 526.95 newtons per volt (53.734 kg per volt), with the zero taken with the chain and strap on (about 0.0870 V in this session; the app sets it itself when you press Zero).
Uncertainty from the electronics at 250 kg: about ±0.14 kg (0.06 %), or ±0.06 kg if you zero before every trial.
  1. Weigh the plates. The electronics are now known to about 0.1 %, so the real masses are the limit. Nothing here proves the plates are as heavy as their labels, and a 1 % error in the plates is a 1 % error in every force. Check a few on a calibrated scale, and weigh the chain and strap.
  2. A second full loop (up and down again) would show whether the +0.29 kg step repeats at the same size, which tells hysteresis from drift. A 10-minute no-load run with the chain on would give the pure drift.
  3. Write the zero-with-rig rule into the SOP: chain and strap on, laptop charging from the isolated outlet, Zero before every trial and a no-load check at the end.
