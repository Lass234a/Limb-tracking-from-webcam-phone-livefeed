# Limb angle tracker (MVIC testing)

> **New to the project, or picking it up from someone else?** Start with `HANDOFF.md`: what this is, where it stands, what was learned from real recordings, what must not be broken, and what is open.

Tracks white or black dots on a live camera feed, shows the joint angles on screen, warns the operator when someone drifts from the set position, and saves the video + angles for each trial.

**Status: version 0.2 (webcam / video file, sagittal view).** It passes its automatic tests on synthetic video. It has **not yet been validated on real footage or against a goniometer** - do that before collecting real data (see "Validation" below).

## Setup (once, on a new computer)
Needs Windows and Python 3.12 (`requirements.txt` includes `pygrabber`, which lists the cameras by name). In this folder:
```
py -3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

## Start it
Double-click **Start.bat**. (First try it without a camera: Live tab -> *Open video file (practice)* -> `demo/demo_leg_white_dots.mp4`.)

## Using it (Live tab)
**A phone camera does not connect?** Double-click `Check_phone_camera.bat`. It tells you in plain English whether the phone's PC program is running and connected, whether the phone can be reached over Wi-Fi, and which stream address works (type it in the stream box). It only reads and saves its result in `phone_camera_check.txt`.
**Force (load cell through an NI DAQ):** in the *Force* box choose *NI DAQ*, check the device (`Dev1` for the USB-6003; *Scan DAQ devices* lists what is connected) and the input (differential: signal+ on `ai0`, signal- on `ai4`, the amplifier's ground on an AI GND pin; single-ended: signal on `ai0`), and press *Connect*. Close MATLAB, NI MAX and other programs that use the DAQ first: only one program can hold it. The load cell's regression (526.95 N per volt, kept in `limbtrack/load_cell_calibrations.json`) is used from the start, so the only step is *Zero* (or the **Z** key) with no pull on the strap, as often as the set-up changes; until the first zero only volts are shown and saved, and the zero is forgotten when the program closes. A zero is accepted when the signal does not drift (the two halves of its 1 s reading within 10 mV) and its random noise is up to 30 mV; the 50 Hz mains hum is not counted against it because it cancels out of the average, and the message after *Zero* says how good the zero is (the noise is saved with each trial). Review's *Calibration* tab shows the regression and how precise a trial's forces are. A new calibration is added with `LoadCellCalibration/logs_to_points.py` and `add_calibration.py` (see the top of each file). The read-out shows newtons, the picture header and the saved overlay video show the force and its peak, and *Force, last 15 s* in the trace menu draws it live. Every recording then also saves `<trial>_force.csv` (all samples) and a `force_n` column; Review draws the force and reports the peak force and the joint angle at the peak. *Simulated* gives a made-up contraction to try everything without hardware. Force needs the live camera (not a video file). Details and limits: `FEATURES.md` section A16.

**Breaks (Pause, key P):** with a camera running, *Pause for a break* stops tracking and shows a pale dashed ghost of the dots as they were. When the participant is back, put the stickers on the dashed circles and press *Resume*; each dot is tracked again once it is back on its circle (nothing is guessed). Not possible while recording; Lock, Record and marking wait until you resume. For a video file the same button plays and pauses it. `FEATURES.md` A19.

**From one trial to the next (0.13.0):** the recording **stops by itself 2 s after the MVIC end marker** (automatic or the E key); the record box counts down and then says *Press R for the next trial*. Untick *Stop the recording by itself after the MVIC end* in *Markers and sound* to stop every recording yourself, or change the 2 s. **Record asks** before it records with a force that has not been zeroed since the program started (such a trial has volts but no newtons). Every action (dots marked, lock, zero, record, stop) is written with its time to `session_log.csv` in the recordings folder, so set-up time can be looked at afterwards; nothing measured is in it. `FEATURES.md` A23.

**Sound cues (Markers and sound section):** beeps for an MVIC: record started; ramp registered (two rising beeps: the MVIC start marker, from the force or the S key); the hold begins one second later (one longer beep) with a short motivating tick every second; relax (two falling beeps) one second before the end; the end marker (one low beep). *Test the beeps* plays them all (and says so if Windows could not play them: check the sound output and volume of the computer). The interval of the ticks and *MVIC length* can be changed; the setting is saved with each trial because beeps may change how a participant pushes. `FEATURES.md` A20.
The right-hand panel has a **strip that never scrolls** and **sections that open one at a time**. The strip has ten chips, one for each step of a session, in the order they are done: **Participant, Camera, Privacy, Position, Dots, Ruler, Lock, Force, Markers, Record**. A tick is done, the amber filled one is what to do next, an amber outline is still to do, grey is not possible yet; click a chip to open its section. **Privacy, Ruler, Force and Markers are asked every session**: they stay amber until they are done or until you tick the "not today" box in their section (*I looked: nothing identifies the participant*, *No ruler today*, *No force today*, *I mark the MVIC by hand today*); these ticks are not remembered, so the question comes back every session. Under the chips is the one line of what to do now, and the buttons used in every trial: **Lock position, Start recording, Pause**. Below are the sections, numbered in the same order (Lock has no section of its own: its button is in the strip and its step opens the Dots section): **1 Participant**, **2 Camera**, **3 Privacy and saved files**, **4 Position and angles** (the session plan, test, target, tolerances, which way the participant faces), **5 Dots, landmarks and lock** (dot colour first, then the list of landmarks), **6 Ruler**, **7 Force**, **8 Markers and sound**, **9 Record**, then the camera set-up checklist and the advanced three-point angle. A closed section says in one line what it is set to, and the panel moves on to the next step by itself when you finish one. Drag the divider between the video and the panel to give either more room.

**Position and dots always in view (0.18.0):** under the Lock / Record / Pause buttons a bar stays on screen whichever section is open. Its **Position** line is a dropdown of the whole session plan with the trials saved for this participant (`Knee extension, hip 90, 70 deg   (1/2)`; hover for which trial is next), with arrows for the previous and next position; picking one sets the test and the target. Under it is one numbered button per landmark (green = marked, amber = LOST, grey = not marked, blue = waiting for your click) and **Redo all (D)** in the last cell (see *Redo all* below). The bar takes about 80 pixels of height from the sections below it, so on a small screen the Camera and Record sections may need a little scrolling. Press a dot's button (or its number key 1-9) and click that dot on the video: only that dot changes. Keys: **D** (F4) redo all dots, **1**-**9** redo one dot, **N** (F3) next position, **B** (F2) previous position; the letters and digits are typed into a text box while it has the focus, the F-keys always work. All of it is switched off while recording. `FEATURES.md` A28.

**Redo all (0.18.1):** press **Redo all (D)** (or **D** / **F4**) after the participant has been moved, and click the dots on the video one after the other, in the order the instruction line asks (*Mark again (3 of 7): click the ... dot*). **The old dots keep tracking until each is replaced**, so nothing changes if you stop: press the button again (it now says *Stop (D)*) and every dot keeps its old mark. The dot being asked for is blue, the ones still to do have a dashed edge, the ones done are solid green. **The lock is released when the first dot has really been marked again** (the participant has been moved: lock again after the last one). A dot that is fine can be **skipped** (press its own button while it is the one asked for), and a dot can be taken **out of turn** (press another dot's button; the pass goes on from the first one still to do). An old mark that has slipped onto a sticker does not stop that sticker being marked (a click on a dot that is *already marked again* is still refused). A recording, a break or a stopped camera ends the pass without touching any dot. Marking the ruler dots and marking dots cannot both wait for your click: starting one ends the other. *Clear all dots and start again* (Dots section) is still there for forgetting every dot first. `FEATURES.md` A29.

**Redo one dot:** in the Dots section every landmark has a row with its state (marked, LOST, not marked, not used) and a button, *Mark* or *Re-mark*. Press it and click that dot on the video: only that dot changes, the others stay as they are, and it keeps waiting until the dot has been found (press the button again, now *Cancel*, to give up). The lock is kept. *Clear all dots and start again* forgets every dot.

**Try the markers without recording:** in *Markers and sound*, with the force connected, press *Try it without recording* and push on the strap as in a real MVIC. The program calls the start from the force, plays the beeps in the 1-5-1 order and says when it would have written the end marker. Nothing is saved and no empty trial is made.

**The program remembers your set-up** (in `settings.json`, this computer only) and opens the last camera, and the force if it was connected, when it starts (a tick box in the Camera section switches that off): the camera by name, test and target, tolerances, dot colour, faces, landmarks, force, MVIC and sound settings, the camera model, the ruler length, the window shape, and the participant code if you used the program in the last 12 hours (it comes back as a question: the chip shows *P07?* until you press Enter in the box, or answer Yes when you press Record). It does **not** remember the ticks of the camera set-up checklist (they are saved into every trial as facts about the camera, so they are made again each session), the marked dots, the lock, the force zero, the ruler marks, **the privacy crop and masks** (set them again each session) or the notes. Delete `settings.json` to start from the defaults. The numbered steps below describe the same things in the order of a first session.
1. **Video source**: pick the camera by name and press *Start camera*. A phone can be used as a camera through a phone-as-webcam app (for example Iriun, DroidCam or Camo, which exist for Android and iPhone): start the app on the phone and on the PC (USB is steadier than Wi-Fi), then press *Scan cameras*; the phone appears in the list like a webcam. Tick the *Camera set-up* list (stabilisation off, zoom 1x, focus/exposure/white balance locked in the phone's app, orientation locked) and press *Check the camera (5 s)* for a plain verdict on frame rate and steadiness; both are saved with each trial. A phone app that publishes a network stream (an address like `http://192.168.1.20:8080/video` or `rtsp://...`) can be used instead: type the address in the *stream address* box and press *Connect to stream* (Wi-Fi adds delay and drops; a camera app over USB is steadier). If the camera isn't listed, press *Scan cameras* (only possible while no camera or video is running, because scanning would switch off a running webcam).
   A line under the buttons shows what the camera really delivers (resolution and measured frame rate) and turns orange with a warning if that differs from the 1280x720, 30 fps the app asks for. The **Lock exposure and white balance** button is switched off (on some webcams it blacks out the picture): use steady lighting, or lock exposure and white balance in the phone or camera app. Webcams are opened on automatic exposure and white balance. **Mirror the image** flips the picture left-right before tracking and recording (saved video and pixel positions are flipped too, `mirrored_image` in `meta.json`); it clears the marked dots, cannot be changed while recording, and after switching it check *Participant faces* against what you now see.
   If something goes wrong in the video thread, the video stops with a message in the instruction line, any recording is saved up to that point, and the details go to `recordings/last_error.log`.
   For video files only: **Pause / Play** (**P**) and single-frame steps back and forward (**Left** / **Right** arrows). You can mark dots, lock, and change targets while paused and the picture updates. At the end of a file it waits on the last frame so you can step back. Stepping is switched off while recording.
2. **Test and target**: choose the test, type the target angle for the main joint (or pick a suggested one; the lowest value of the box is *no target*, which shows the main joint without judging it, as in the belt squat), and set tolerances. Pick the dot colour and which way the participant faces in the image (for black discs drawn on white tape, the planned stickers, choose **Black dots**, not Auto: see `MARKERS.md`; Auto is for white dots on dark cloth and black dots on skin in one picture). The smoothing tick box averages the *displayed* angles over 0.2 s; it never touches what is saved.
   Changing the test keeps the dots you have marked (when the landmarks are the same) and your tolerances; only the lock is dropped, so lock again for the new position.
   **Landmarks** (in the Dots section): tick or untick which of the 7 landmarks you will put dots on, or use a preset such as "only Knee flexion". Angles that need an unticked landmark disappear (the panel lists which are available and what the others need); the rest keep working. If the main joint's landmarks are unticked, no target is judged and every remaining angle is checked against the locked position instead. Your choice stays while you switch tests, is saved with each trial (`dots_disabled` in `meta.json`, and the CSV only has the chosen dots and angles), and is forgotten when the program is closed. It cannot be changed during a recording; re-enabling a landmark means marking it again.
   **Participant faces check**: the program compares the *Participant faces* setting with what the marked dots show (either leg, mirror on or off): from the four foot dots, or, when they are not all used, from the knee when it is bent more than 25 deg (a knee bends one way only). **Lying face down usually needs the other side than sitting or lying on the back.** If they disagree, the header shows a CHECK message, the box turns amber and Lock is refused until you correct the setting.
   **Three-point angle**: tick *Use a three-point angle* and choose any three landmarks; the app shows the unsigned angle at the middle one and switches those three landmarks on. It is shown as the interior angle (a straight limb reads 180 deg) or, if you tick *Show it as flexion*, as flexion (a straight limb reads 0 deg, like the protocol's targets); the target is converted when you switch, and in the interior convention the test's flexion positions are not offered, so a flexion target is never compared with an interior angle. By default it becomes the main joint, so the target above applies to it (the test's own angles are then checked against the locked position instead); untick *Use it as the main joint* to show it alongside the others. This works with as few as three dots, for example trochanter, epicondyle and malleolus when no fibular-head or foot dots are available.
**Scale (optional, recommended)**: put a ruler of 10 to 20 cm at the same distance from the camera as the joint, square to the camera, with a dot sticker centred on each end mark. In the *Ruler* section type the length between the two dot centres, press *Mark the two ruler dots* and click both dots. The app shows the scale (pixels per cm), draws the ruler in the picture, in the saved overlay video and in Review, and warns if the ruler is very short in the picture. The two ruler dots are fixed points: they are not tracked, so shadows and hands cannot lose them, but if the camera or the ruler is moved you must mark the ruler again. The scale is saved in `meta.json` (`scale`) and per frame in the CSV (`scale_px_per_cm`). With a scale set, the box also lists the approximate width of the smallest dot and warns (**TOO SMALL**) about any dot under the protocol minimum of 2 cm; this is an estimate from the size the dot had when you marked it, so check it once against stickers of known size. It is only right for things at the ruler's distance from the camera; lens distortion is not corrected. It cannot be changed while recording, and a new video source, the mirror switch or a change of dot colour clears it.
**Strap and anchor dots (optional)**: tick *strap marker* (a dot on the strap where it attaches to the person) and *anchor marker* (a dot at the fixed end of the strap, rope or chain; on the rope itself if the anchor is out of the picture) in the Landmarks box, or use the preset *all landmarks + strap and anchor*. They are marked, tracked and recorded like the landmarks (a magenta line joins them in the picture), but they belong to no angle, so they never affect an angle or block Lock. Keep the strap dot at least about three dot-widths from the malleolus dot. With both dots on, the picture header shows the **angle of pull** (the angle between the rope, from the strap marker towards the anchor marker, and the segment it pulls on: shank in knee tests, thigh in hip tests, foot in ankle tests; 90 deg = square to the segment) and the **moment arm** (shortest distance from the joint to the line of pull, in cm if a ruler scale is set). They are recorded per frame in the CSV (`pull_angle_deg`, `pull_off90_deg`, `moment_arm_px/_cm`, `strap_joint_px/_cm`; blank while a needed dot is lost), never judged, and never change an angle. This is the 2D picture only: a pull toward or away from the camera is invisible.
   If two labels look swapped or doubled (a sticker clicked twice, foot dots the wrong way round) the header shows CHECK LABELS and Lock is refused until you re-mark.
3. **Mark dots**: click each dot on the video when asked. The seven landmarks follow your goniometer table: the iliac crest (the point on the side of the pelvis halfway between ASIS and PSIS), greater trochanter, lateral femoral epicondyle, fibular head, lateral malleolus, 5th metatarsal base and head. Click the centre. To fix one dot later, click it again (a lost dot is fixed by clicking where it really is; the app says which dot it re-marked, and refuses to put a dot onto its neighbour). Clicks on the video are ignored while recording. Then position the participant. The main joint turns **green** inside target +/- tolerance, **red** outside.
   **Redo one dot**: press the *Re-mark* button in that landmark's row and click the dot (see above). The other dots stay as they are.
4. **Lock position** (button or **Space**) once they are in position. The other joints' current angles become their references, and from then on they go red if they drift more than the "other joints" tolerance. (The *ghost* of the locked pose that earlier versions drew is switched off in this version; see `PARKED.md`.)
   **Trails**: while you record, each dot draws a thin coloured path of where it has been (a gap where it was lost). The trails start when you press Start recording, stay on screen after you stop, and start afresh at the next recording. They appear in the live view, the saved overlay video and the Review tab (tick box *Show trails*, which only changes what is drawn; no number is affected). A dot adds a trail point only after moving at least 0.5 px.
5. **Record** (button or **R**): enter a participant ID first. **Lock the position before you record.** During a recording the test, target, tolerances, dot colour, "Participant faces", mirror, landmarks, ruler scale, *Clear all dots and start again* and the Lock button are greyed out and refused by the program, so a trial's settings cannot change halfway; the saved `meta.json` reports the settings as they were at the start. The mouse wheel no longer changes a drop-down or number box unless you have clicked into it. Record is refused if no video is coming in or a video file has already ended. If frames stop arriving during a recording the label turns red ("NO VIDEO"); a live camera that is silent for 2 s is closed and opened again, and only after 6 s is the session ended, and any stretch without frames is listed in `meta.json` (`gaps`) and in the Review title, and after Stop it tells you how many frames were saved (an empty or very short trial is called out). During the trial press **S** (MVIC start), **E** (MVIC end) or **M** (numbered marker), **or let the program mark the MVIC from the force**: with the force connected, tick *Mark the MVIC automatically from the force* in the Record box (and set *Start when force rises by*, default 10 N, and *MVIC length*, default 7 s = the protocol's 1-5-1 tempo). Pressing Record arms it (the picture and the Record box say *AUTO MVIC: armed, waiting for the ramp*, so press Record while the person is at rest). When the force clearly leaves its rest level the **MVIC start** marker is written (about 0.15 to 0.2 s after the ramp began), and the **MVIC end** marker 7 s later; they are the same markers as S and E, and pressing S or E by hand takes over. If the force never rises, nothing is marked and Review's Check tab says so. It changes no measured number (`FEATURES.md` A18). **While a text box (Participant ID, Notes) has focus, letter keys are typed into it, not used as shortcuts** (an orange hint appears): press **Enter** or **Esc** to leave the box, or use the function keys, which always work: **F5** record, **F6** MVIC start, **F7** MVIC end, **F8** marker, **F9** lock, **F4** redo all dots, **F3** / **F2** next / previous position (a USB foot pedal can be set to send these). Each marker is confirmed under the recording timer ("MVIC start marked at 12.3 s") with a running count of starts, ends and other markers. Each trial is saved in `recordings/<participant>/`:
   - `..._raw.mp4` - untouched video (opens in Kinovea)
   - `..._overlay.mp4` - video with the angles, trails and marker banners drawn on (optional: untick *Also save the overlay video* to make recording lighter; the Review tab's *Export overlay video* makes the same video later from the raw video and the CSV)
   - `..._data.csv` - one row per frame (columns below; `frame` counts from 0 and is the frame number in the two videos, `source_frame` is the frame number in the source)
   - (every CSV row is saved as it is recorded and `..._meta.json` exists from the first frame, so a crash still leaves a reviewable trial; if the camera changes picture size the recording stops and is saved up to there)
   - `..._meta.json` - participant, test, target, tolerances, facing, mirror, smoothing, camera and its settings, every tracker number (`tracker_settings`), notes, markers

While the video runs, the **live trace** under it shows the last 15 s: the main angle with its tolerance band, or (switch at the top) the drift of all joints from their set position, which is the best view for spotting slow creep.

### What the screen colours mean
| Where | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Angle text / limb lines green | inside tolerance |
| Angle text / limb lines red | outside tolerance |
| Angle text white | no reference yet (not locked) |
| Dot ring yellow | dot strong |
| Dot ring orange + "weak dot (fading)" | dot contrast has dropped below 60 % of when it was marked: fix lighting/glare before it is lost |
| Dot grey with a cross, angle "lost" | dot not found; nothing is guessed |

### CSV columns
`frame, t_s` (seconds from trial start), `clock_s` (camera clock; force samples use the same clock), `locked`, then per dot `<dot>_x, <dot>_y, <dot>_lost, <dot>_q` (quality 0-1; x and y are blank while the dot is lost), then per angle `<angle>_deg` (the unsmoothed angle), `<angle>_ref`, `<angle>_dev`, `<angle>_ok` (1 in tolerance, 0 out, blank = no reference / lost; all computed from the unsmoothed angle), then `all_ok` and `event` (marker label on the frame where it was pressed).

## Review tab
Open a trial: since 0.14.0 the graphs come first. The video and its play controls are top left, the angle graph with the force graph under it (same time axis) top right, and the numbers in a wide strip below in tabs (*Check* is a plain go / no-go list for the trial; *Angles*; *Force* with peak, plateau, timing, steadiness and torque; *Calibration*; *Dots and pull*). The yellow line on the graphs is where the video is. For a trial with force, the switch **Graphs: Force against angle** (since 0.17.0) draws the joint's angle against the force, picture by picture, from purple (start) to yellow (end), so the shift of the joint under load is one look; the star is the peak force. The marker list, the two *Window = ...* buttons and the force settings sit under the graphs; the export buttons are in the top bar. Both dividers can be dragged and are remembered. Drag the blue band to choose the window, or use *Window = MVIC start to end* / *Window = force above half of peak*. *Export all trials of this participant* writes one row per trial and flags two trials of the same position that differ by more than 10 %.

Details of the older parts:
*Open trial...* -> pick a `..._meta.json`. Scrub or play the video with its overlay (including the trails), see angle-vs-time (unsmoothed, as saved) with the green tolerance band and the markers as dashed vertical lines. Pick a marker in the list to jump to it. **Window = MVIC start to end** sets the analysis window to your markers; otherwise drag the blue window on the plot. The numbers below use the window and the unsmoothed saved angles (so the SD includes tracking noise). A second block gives **the angle during the hold** (middle 60 % of the window = plateau, median, the rest angle in the second before, the shift from rest to hold, and the target), because the angle reached during the contraction is not the typed one. The exported summary starts with participant, test, trial and software-version columns so trials can be combined. First block: mean, SD, min, max, **start>end** (how far the angle moved between the start and the end of the window, the slack take-up number), max deviation, % time in tolerance, lost frames. *Export summary CSV* saves them, and also writes `..._displacement.csv`. The window opens on your MVIC start-to-end markers when you pressed both (otherwise the whole trial). Under the numbers, **Dot movement** lists how far every dot moved from its starting position in the window (end shift and largest shift, in pixels and, if a ruler scale was set, in cm; and relative to the anchor dot if it was used, which removes a bumped camera but not a strap sliding on the skin). If strap and anchor dots were used there is also a **Pull** table (pull angle, moment arm, strap-to-joint distance: mean, min, max and change from start to end).

## Changing tests, angles and conventions
Edit `protocol.json` (plain text). The program checks the file every time it starts and refuses to open with a plain-English list of every mistake and where it is (misspelt dot or direction, a number written as text, a tolerance of zero or less, a main joint that is not one of the test's angles, and so on, with a "did you mean" hint). To check your edits without starting the program: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m limbtrack.protocol check`. An angle is the angle between two arms: an arm is a vector between two dots, "perpendicular to a line between two dots", or a fixed direction ("up", "down", "forward"). Shown value = `sign` x angle + `offset`; `signed` angles can be negative. Landmarks and arms follow Tabel 3 (goniometer alignment):
- hip = pelvis midline (iliac crest dot -> trochanter, the goniometer's lateral midline of the pelvis) vs femur (trochanter -> epicondyle); flexion +, extension -
- knee = femur vs fibula (fibular head -> malleolus); 0 = straight
- ankle = fibula vs 5th metatarsal (base -> head); dorsiflexion +, 90 deg between the arms = 0
- (there is no pelvic tilt angle since 0.9.0: it needs two pelvis dots, and the pelvis now has one)
- **hip abduction / adduction (frontal view, since 0.15.0)**, camera in front of the participant, three dots: the ASIS of the tested leg, the ASIS of the other side, the centre of the patella. Two angles per test, both positive for the test's own direction (abduction: the knee moves away from the other leg; adduction: towards it). *Goniometer angle*: Tabel 3 as written, fulcrum the ASIS, one arm towards the other ASIS, the other arm to the patella centre; 0 = the thigh line square to the line between the ASIS. This is the one judged (green/red) against 0 / 20 deg on the screen. *About the hip centre* (shown and saved, never judged): the same thigh line drawn from the **computed hip joint centre**, the point that really rotates. The hip centre is calculated in every picture from the two ASIS and the ruler scale with the Harrington et al. (2007) equation (set 1: sideways 0.33 x ASIS-to-ASIS distance + 7.3 mm, down 0.30 x distance + 10.9 mm, from the midpoint of the two ASIS), drawn as a cyan diamond, and saved as `hip_centre_x`, `hip_centre_y` in the CSV, so nothing has to be calculated afterwards. **The two angles do not share a zero**: with an average pelvis and a 40 cm thigh the goniometer reads 0 when the thigh is about 5 deg out about the hip centre, and 20 when it is about 29 deg out. It needs the ruler (the equation is in millimetres); without it the hip-centre angle stays blank. The equation's constants were typed from a secondary source and not yet checked against the original table (see `limbtrack/geometry.py`). Which way the participant faces does not matter; you choose the tested leg instead. Hip flexion 0 and the straight knee are not measured in these tests: the participant stands against a wall (heel, buttocks, back touching) and you tick that box.

Tests, positions and required neighbour angles follow Tabel 2 of the protocol draft of 2026-09-28 (15 sagittal positions, and since 0.15.0 the 4 hip abduction/adduction positions with the camera in front: 0 and 20 deg each): knee extension (hip 90) 20/70/110; knee flexion (hip 90) 20/90; hip extension with a straight knee 0/45 and with the knee at 90 0/90; hip flexion (knee 90) 0/90; ankle plantarflexion and dorsiflexion (knee straight) 0/-20. The neighbouring joint's required angle (hip 90, knee 0 or knee 90) is judged from the start, with the "other joints" tolerance, not only after Lock; the other joints are still compared with their value at Lock. The knee angle is signed: hyperextension reads negative. Where the draft contradicts itself (section 2.7 lists -20/0/20 for the ankle) Tabel 2 was used, because it gives the stated total of 19 positions. Signed angles assume which way the participant faces, so set "Participant faces" correctly. **Check all of this against your final protocol.**

## Where the numbers can go wrong (read this)
- The angle is the **2D angle in the image**. It is only the true joint angle if the limb moves in a plane facing the camera. Put the camera level, at joint height, square to the sagittal plane, and don't let the participant rotate.
- Dots on skin are not the joint centres. Place them the same way every time (same person, palpated landmarks). The hip is the hardest.
- Dots on straps can slide over the skin without the joint moving (and the reverse). Validate strap-mounted dots separately; prefer dots on skin or tape in the gaps where you can.
- The display smoothing is a trailing average over 0.2 s, so a steadily changing angle is shown about 0.1 s late (about 5 deg at 50 deg/s, 0.1 deg at 1 deg/s). Untick it to see the raw angle. The saved CSV is never smoothed, so the green/red on screen can differ slightly from the `_ok` column.
- Lens distortion is not corrected yet. Keep the participant near the centre of the frame.
- A dot that is hidden (hand, strap) is frozen where it was last seen and its angles go blank; nothing is guessed. It picks up again only if a round dot of about the right size and brightness reappears within about two dot-widths of that spot; otherwise click it again. The video it was tuned on had dots smaller than planned, so test this with your real dots (planned size: at least 2 cm).
- Dot tracking assumes the dots are clearly brighter (white) or darker (black) than what's around them. Good even lighting and matte dots help. Glare on shiny dots or clothing seams near a dot can confuse it. Dots closer together than about 3 dot-widths can be confused.

## Validation (do once, and again if the camera setup changes)
1. **Goniometer check**: set the limb to 5+ angles per joint (e.g. 30/60/90/120/150 deg), hold still ~5 s each while recording (use S / E markers). Since 0.16.0 the reading can be typed in: in the Live tab's *Reference reading* section press **Reading taken now (G)** as you read the goniometer or inclinometer, type what it said, then record. Review's *Reference* tab shows the reading beside the app's angle at that moment, and *Reference validation...* gives the average difference, spread and an agreement plot over all trials. Aim for agreement within about 2-3 deg with a hand goniometer (it cannot show better).
2. **Kinovea check**: open a `..._raw.mp4` in Kinovea, measure the angle with its angle tool at a few moments, and compare with the CSV at the same times.
3. **Noise floor**: record a still participant (or a mannequin) for 60 s; the SD in Review is the tracking noise. It should be well under your tolerance.
4. **Stress it**: cover a dot with a hand, change the lighting, and confirm the dot goes orange/grey and comes back correctly.
