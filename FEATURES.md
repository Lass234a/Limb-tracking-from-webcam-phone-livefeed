# Feature inventory

Version 0.7.0 (`meta.json` now stores the version, a `meta_schema`, and a `provenance` block: code hash, git commit, library versions, computer, protocol-file hash). First written 2026-10-02 at commit `77c4d7d`; many sections updated since, see CHANGELOG.md. Function names are given instead of line numbers because lines move.

**Purpose of the app:** show a live view of joint positions and angles from marker dots on bone landmarks, so each participant (maximal isometric contractions, strapped into a harness with an S-type load cell) can be placed in the correct position quickly. A webcam is the current proof of concept. The hip abduction/adduction tests use the same camera moved to the front (A25, 0.15.0); two cameras at once (frontal and sagittal) are parked.

**Status:** everything below is tested on synthetic video, plus one real practice video (`Bundepus.mp4`, which is not in the repository) for the lost-dot rule and the partly-covered-dot flag, and stand-ins for cameras, phones and a network stream. There are 378 automatic tests (1 skipped: the parked ghost). Nothing has been validated against a goniometer or inclinometer (see `SETUP_AND_VALIDATION.md`), no phone has been tried, and the dots in the practice video are smaller than the planned 2 cm.

**What the app does NOT do to the data:** no interpolation of missing dots or angles, no drift correction, no outlier rejection (other than the lost-dot rule), no frame-rate resampling, no lens-distortion correction, no camera-tilt correction. A pixel-to-centimetre scale exists only if you mark a ruler (section F).

**What changed since the first inventory (2026-10-01), following your decisions:** the growing search and the old lost-dot rule were replaced (A6); the One-Euro filter was replaced by a 0.2 s moving average that only changes the screen (A7); the ghost pose was commented out (A13); then a three-point angle mode, trails, a ruler scale, strap and anchor dots with the angle of pull and moment arm, dot displacement, the hold-angle report, Tabel 2 protocol positions, camera handling for webcams, phones and streams, recording safety and many checks were added. `CHANGELOG.md` lists them in one place.

**Legend**
- **Who:** *You* = you asked for it or decided it. *Approved* = I proposed it and you approved. *Added* = I added it without discussing it.
- **Need:** *Essential* = needed to place a participant live. *Helpful* = optional but useful live. *Extra* = data logging or review; requested for the study, but not needed for live positioning.

## A. Features that alter or define the measured numbers

These are the ones to justify in a methods section. **Every number in A1 to A5 is also written into each trial's `meta.json` under `tracker_settings`**, and a test (`tests/test_tracker_settings.py`) fails if tracking output changes, so a recording can be traced to the exact settings that made it. Constants live at the top of `limbtrack/tracker.py`.

### A1. Marker snapping at marking
- **What:** clicking a dot moves the marker to the nearest real dot instead of using the click point.
- **Problem / who:** gives an exact starting centre from an imprecise click. *Added; you chose to keep it.*
- **Alters numbers:** yes, the starting position and the dot's reference size and contrast.
  - An 81x81 px window around the click is blurred (3x3) and top-hat filtered with a 41 px square kernel (dot diameter assumed 16 px).
  - The strongest response within 8 px of the click must be at least 8 grey levels, otherwise marking fails.
  - Blobs above 50 % of that peak are taken; the one whose centre is nearest the click wins.
  - It is rejected if that centre is more than 15 px from the click.
  - The blob area sets the dot's reference area (diameter = 2*sqrt(area/pi), minimum 4 px) and its peak sets the reference contrast.
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `DotTracker.init_at`; settings `image_preparation.marking_*`.

### A2. Frame-to-frame snapping (the tracker)
- **What:** every frame, the tracker searches near where the dot should be and snaps to the best-matching blob.
- **Alters numbers:** yes, it defines the position of every dot in every frame.
  - Predicted position = last position + velocity; velocity = 0.5 x (new - old position).
  - Search reach = `min(3 x diam + 12, 160)` px; the window extends `diam` beyond that. The reach no longer grows while a dot is lost (see A6).
  - Detection threshold = `max(0.4 x contrast, 8)` grey levels; `contrast` is a running average (0.95 old + 0.05 new).
  - A blob is accepted only if its area is 0.40 to 3.5 times the running area (running area = 0.9 old + 0.1 new; the lower limit was 0.3 until 2026-10-09, when small fragments of a dot hidden by a hand were accepted as the dot) and its longer side is at most `2.5 x shorter side + 2`.
  - Of the accepted blobs, the one nearest the predicted position is used.
  - **Dot merged with a shadow, finger or strap (2026-10-09):** if no blob is accepted, or the best one is bigger than 1.5 x the area when marked, or longer than `1.6 x shorter side + 1`, the search is repeated at 0.6, 0.75 and 0.9 x the dot's contrast and the first acceptable blob that is no longer merged is used. The running dot area is no longer updated from a blob bigger than 1.5 x the marked area. Found on the board recordings: before this, the position was dragged toward the finger and the dot was then lost until the finger left. On the Bundepus reference video this moves positions by at most 0.27 px (angles by 0.02 deg) in 40 of 312 frames; the synthetic reference is identical.
- **Who:** *You* (live tracking); the method was *Added*.
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `DotTracker.update`; settings `tracking.*`.

### A3. Image preparation for detection
- **What:** the frame is converted to grey (OpenCV weighted average), blurred 3x3, then a top-hat (white dots) or black-hat (dark dots) removes the background.
- **Problem / who:** large bright or dark areas (shoes, clothes) are ignored. *Added; you chose to keep it.*
- **Alters numbers:** yes, slightly: the blur softens the image before the position is measured. The kernel is a square of side `odd(max(2.5 x diam, 9))` px.
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `_enhance`, `to_gray`; settings `image_preparation.*`.

### A4. Sub-pixel dot centre
- **What:** the dot position is the intensity-weighted mean of the blob's pixels, weighted by their top-hat value.
- **Alters numbers:** yes; this is the measurement itself. Only pixels above the A2 threshold count, so uneven lighting across a dot can bias the centre slightly. On synthetic dots the error was under 0.4 px.
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `_blobs`.

### A5. Neighbour exclusion
- **What:** a tracker ignores blobs within `0.8 x diam` of where another tracked dot is **expected** this frame (its last position plus its step, with the circle widened by the length of that step), so a fast-moving neighbour cannot be taken by a covered dot (audit TRK-01). A second rule handles two trackers ending up within `0.5 x diam` of each other: the one that fits its own expectation worse (distance from its predicted position, or from where it froze if it was lost) gives the dot up, is put back at its previous position (`undo_update`) and declared lost; on a tie the later one. Normal tracking output is unchanged (checked against the saved reference run and `Bundepus.mp4`); a dot moving faster than about 360 deg/s may now be lost instead of mis-tracked.
- **Problem / who:** when a dot is hidden, its tracker could jump onto a neighbouring dot (this happened in a test). *Added (bug fix); you chose to keep it.*
- **Alters numbers:** yes; it changes which blob a dot is allowed to take.
- **Need:** Helpful, important when dots sit close together.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `TrackerSet.update`; settings `tracking.neighbour_exclusion_factor`, `duplicate_factor`.

### A6. Lost-dot rule (replaces the old rule and the growing search)
- **What:** if no acceptable blob is found, the dot is flagged lost and **frozen** at its last good position. There is no velocity prediction while it is lost. Only a **fixed circle** of 2.0 x the dot's diameter (measured when it was lost) is searched, and it never grows.
- **Resuming** needs a convincing dot inside that circle, not just anything dark or bright:
  - contrast at least 0.6 x the contrast at marking,
  - area between 0.5 and 2.0 x the learned area,
  - roughly round: longer side at most `1.6 x shorter side + 1`.
  - A dot that moved further than the circle while hidden stays lost until you click it again.
- **Alters numbers:**
  - Any angle using a lost dot is blank ("lost"); it is never guessed or interpolated.
  - **The CSV writes blank x and y while a dot is lost** (the frozen position is not written). `_lost = 1` marks it.
- **Evidence:** on the real practice video the old rule locked onto a heel shadow 60 px away for 70 frames. The new rule with only a brightness check locked onto a dark table-leg sliver; the size and shape checks fixed that. The circle size was tried at several values on that video. At the default 2.0 diameters, a dot that reappears further away stays lost (safe, but you must click it again). At 8 to 12 diameters (46 to 70 px) the dot resumed correctly; at 16 or more (93 px and up) the tracker locked onto other dark objects. The default was kept at 2.0 until a proper test video exists.
- **Who:** *You* (your decision on 2026-10-01).
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `tracker.py` `DotTracker.update`; `engine.py` `_compute_angles`; settings `lost_dot.*`; tests `tests/test_lost_rule.py`.

### A7. Display smoothing (replaces the One-Euro filter)
- **What:** one fixed trailing moving average over 0.2 s, with an on/off tick box (default on).
- **Alters numbers: on the screen only.** The displayed angle, the colours, the live trace and the overlay video use the smoothed value. **The CSV (`_deg`, `_ref`, `_dev`, `_ok`, `all_ok`) always uses the unsmoothed angle**, so what you see can differ slightly from the CSV verdict.
  - A steadily changing angle is shown about 0.1 s late (about 5 deg at 50 deg/s, 0.1 deg at 1 deg/s).
  - The average restarts after a gap where an angle could not be computed (lost dot) and if time stops increasing (stepping backwards).
  - The setting is written to `meta.json` (`smoothing`).
- **Who:** *You.*
- **Need:** Helpful, optional.
- **Code:** `filters.py` `MovingAverage`; `engine.py` `_compute_angles`, `set_smoothing`.

### A8a. Participant-faces check (new)
- **What:** the program works out which side of the body the marked dots show, from the turning direction of the foot dots (malleolus, fibular head, metatarsal base and head; all four must be marked; until 0.9.0 the pelvis dots ASIS, PSIS and trochanter were used first, but one iliac crest dot has no direction of its own), **or, since 0.13.0, when the foot dots are not all marked, from the bend of the knee** (thigh trochanter -> epicondyle against shank fibular head -> malleolus; used only when bent more than 25 deg either way, `KNEE_FACING_MIN_DEG`, more than any knee over-straightens; P11 2026-10-15: two face-down trials with *faces right* read the knee as -55 and -78 deg and nothing warned; over all 23 real recordings it flags exactly those two), and compares it with the *Participant faces* setting. This works for either leg, with the mirror on or off, and for upright, seated or lying postures. If they disagree for 5 frames in a row, the header says "CHECK 'Participant faces' ...", the setting's box turns amber, and **Lock is refused** with the reason (the hip sign would be wrong). `meta.json` records `facing` and `facing_detected`. With only three dots (three-point mode) there is nothing to check. Record is not blocked, but Lock is the step before it.
- **Code:** `geometry.py` `view_handedness` (`foot_handedness`, then `knee_bend_handedness`); `engine.py` `_check_facing`, `facing_mismatch`, `facing_message`, `lock_problem`.

### A8. Angle definition and conventions
- **What:** an angle is measured between two "arms". An arm is a vector between two dots, "perpendicular to the line between two dots pointing toward the feet" (supported, but no angle uses it since 0.9.0; the hip's pelvis arm is the vector from the iliac crest dot to the trochanter), or a fixed direction (up, down, forward).
- **Alters numbers:** yes.
  - Shown value = `sign x angle + offset`. Hip: signed, sign -1 (flexion +, extension -). Knee: signed (flexion +, hyperextension -). Ankle: unsigned, sign -1, offset +90 (dorsiflexion +).
  - If "participant faces left" is chosen, the x-direction of dot-based arms is mirrored so signs mean the same either way. A wrong setting flips the sign of the hip without any warning; knee and ankle are unaffected. "Faces" refers to the picture on screen, so after switching the mirror (E3) it must be set again.
- **Who:** *You* (landmarks; on 2026-10-15 you replaced ASIS and PSIS by one iliac crest dot halfway between them, and the hip's pelvis arm became the line from that dot to the trochanter); the facing setting and sign/offset mechanism were *Added*.
- **Need:** Essential.
- **Code:** `geometry.py` `resolve_arm`, `vector_angle`, `to_display`; `angles.py` `measure`; `protocol.json`.

### A9. Three-point angle
- **Two conventions (audit ANG-05):** *interior angle* (default; a straight limb reads 180, a knee flexed 70 reads 110) or, with the tick box *Show it as flexion*, **flexion = 180 minus the interior angle** (a straight limb reads 0, like the protocol's flexion angles and targets). The convention is in the angle's name on screen and in `meta.json` (`three_point_convention`). The typed target never silently changes meaning: switching the convention converts it (110 interior becomes 70 flexion), and in the interior convention the test's own flexion positions are not offered or carried over (the target starts as *no target*). Because the protocol targets are flexion, the flexion convention is the one to use when the protocol positions apply. The three-point knee (trochanter, epicondyle, malleolus) is a few degrees different from the protocol knee (which uses the fibular head), by how far the fibular head sits off the epicondyle-malleolus line.
- **What:** choose any three landmarks and the app shows the unsigned interior angle at the middle one (a straight limb reads 180 deg). It switches those three landmarks on, and by default becomes the main joint so the typed target applies to it; the test's own angles are then checked against the locked position instead. A tick box shows it alongside the other angles instead.
- **Problem / who:** only three dots are available in some pilot set-ups; the knee angle needs four. *You.*
- **Alters numbers:** it adds one angle; the other definitions are unchanged. Saved as `three_point` in `meta.json` and as an extra angle column group in the CSV.
- **Need:** Helpful (pilot).
- **Code:** `geometry.py` `interior_angle`; `engine.py` `set_three_point`, `clear_three_point`; `gui.py` three-point panel.

### A10. Reference, deviation and tolerance
- **What:** decides whether an angle is "in position".
- **Alters numbers:** the verdicts and deviation columns.
  - Main joint: `deviation = value - target`, where the target is what the operator types.
  - Neighbour joints with a required angle in the protocol (Tabel 2: hip 90, knee 0 or knee 90): that angle, from the start. Other joints: after Lock, each is compared with the **unsmoothed angle averaged over the 0.2 s before Lock**. This is the same whether the display smoothing is on or off (audit DOC-02), so the saved `_ref` and `locked_references_deg` do not depend on that switch.
  - In tolerance when `|deviation| <= tolerance` (inclusive, no hysteresis). Defaults: 3 deg main joint, 5 deg other joints; both adjustable.
  - The CSV `_dev` and `_ok` are computed from the unsmoothed angle against these references.
- **Who:** *You* (you chose to keep it); the design was *Added*.
- **Need:** Helpful.
- **Code:** `engine.py` `lock_position`, `_compute_angles`.

### A11. Timestamps and video speed
- **What:** time base for the CSV and videos.
- **Alters numbers:** timing only.
  - Live camera: a frame's time is the PC clock when it was read (not the camera's own clock). `t_s` counts from the first recorded frame; `clock_s` is the raw clock.
  - Video file: time = frame number / the file's frame rate.
  - The raw and overlay videos are tagged with the **source's own frame rate**: a video file's rate, or a camera's measured arrival rate (falling back to the requested rate, then to an estimate from the last frame times, always between 1 and 240 fps). Redrawing the same frame, stepping back and switching source no longer distort it (audit CR-06, CAM-03). If the computer cannot keep up while recording, the video can still play faster than real time. **The CSV `t_s` is the authoritative time.**
  - CSV `frame` is the 0-based row number, which equals the frame index in `raw.mp4` and `overlay.mp4`; the new last column `source_frame` is the frame number in the source (the file's frame, or the camera's read count) (audit CR-08).
- **Need:** Helpful; essential if force is added later.
- **Code:** `camera.py` `CameraSource.read`; `engine.py` `_fps`.

### A12. Review-tab statistics
- **What:** the summary numbers for a chosen time window.
- **Alters numbers:** computed, not measured. **They are now computed from the CSV, so they use the unsmoothed angles.**
  - Mean, SD (sample SD, ddof = 1), min, max, max |deviation|, % of frames in tolerance (among frames that have a verdict), lost frames.
  - `start>end` = mean of the last 5 valid values minus mean of the first 5 valid values in the window.
  - The SD therefore includes tracking noise; the smoothing no longer hides it.
- **Who:** *You* (review tab kept).
- **Need:** Extra.
- **Code:** `review.py` `Trial.summary`.

### A12a. The angle during the hold (audit VAL-01)
- **Why:** the joint angle changes between positioning and the contraction (in the literature by about 19 deg for the knee against a rigid arm), so the typed target is not the angle at which the moment was produced. The angle to report is the one measured **during the hold**.
- **What the Review tab and the exported summary now give per angle** (all from the unsmoothed CSV angle, for the analysis window, which opens on the MVIC start-to-end markers): `plateau_mean_deg` (mean over the middle 60 % of the window: the first and last 20 % are left out as ramps), `median_deg`, `rest_mean_deg` (mean over the 1 s before the window, at least 5 valid frames), `rest_to_hold_shift_deg` (plateau minus rest), `target_deg` (the typed target for the main joint, the reference for the others) and `mean_minus_target_deg`. A window too short for a plateau or rest gives a blank (NaN), never a guess.
- **The export** now starts with `participant, test_key, trial, tolerance_deg, software_version, window_source` so the summaries of a whole study can be put in one table; `window_source` says whether the window was the MVIC markers or set by hand. Without markers the screen says so.
- **Which number goes to AnyBody is your decision.** Both are now reported side by side: the plateau mean angle, and (with force recorded) the main joint angle at the peak force (mean of the video frames within 0.1 s of the peak; section A16).

### A12b. Dot displacement in Review (new)
- **What:** for the chosen analysis window, the Review tab shows how far every recorded dot moved, and the export writes `<trial>_displacement.csv` next to the summary CSV.
  - **end shift:** mean of the dot's last 5 valid positions minus the mean of its first 5 valid positions in the window (a vector dx, dy and its length), in pixels and, with a ruler scale, in cm.
  - **max shift:** the largest distance from the starting position at any frame in the window. A single bad tracking frame shows up here; check the trails.
  - **vs anchor:** the same numbers for the dot's position relative to the anchor marker (when it was used). This removes movement of the whole picture (a camera that was bumped). It does **not** separate the person shifting from a strap sliding on the skin; for the strap, use the pull numbers.
  - **Pull section** (only when strap and anchor dots were used): mean, min, max and start>end for the pull angle, moment arm and strap-to-joint distance (cm with a scale).
- **Start of the hold:** the window now **opens on the MVIC start-to-end window when both markers were pressed**, otherwise on the whole trial, so the displacement (and all Review statistics) start at the hold by default. Drag the blue window or press *Window = MVIC start to end* to change it. (This changes the default window of the existing Review statistics too.)
- **Alters numbers:** none are measured; all are computed afterwards from the CSV positions (and the scale in `meta.json`). Lost frames are skipped, never filled in. Image pixels are the picture's, so displacement in cm is only right at the ruler's distance from the camera.
- **Code:** `limbtrack/displacement.py`; `review.py` `Trial.displacement`, `pull_summary`, `ReviewTab._displacement_text`, `export`.

### A15. Angle of pull and moment arm (strap and anchor dots)
- **What:** when both the strap marker and the anchor marker are switched on, the app shows and records, every frame:
  - **pull angle** (`pull_angle_deg`): the angle between the direction of pull (from the strap marker towards the anchor marker) and the segment the strap acts on, measured along the segment from its proximal to its distal dot. 90 deg = square to the segment; below 90 the pull has a component towards the feet, above 90 towards the body. `pull_off90_deg` is the same minus 90.
  - **moment arm** (`moment_arm_px`, `moment_arm_cm`): shortest distance from the joint dot to the line of pull.
  - **strap-joint distance** (`strap_joint_px`, `strap_joint_cm`): straight distance from the joint dot to the strap marker.
- **Which segment and joint:** the second arm and the fulcrum of the test's main joint: knee tests use the shank (fibular head to malleolus) and the epicondyle, hip tests the thigh (trochanter to epicondyle) and the trochanter, ankle tests the foot (5th metatarsal base to head) and the malleolus.
- **Alters numbers:** it adds columns only; no angle or dot position changes (checked by a test). Nothing is judged and nothing blocks Lock or Record. The pull is shown as a line in the picture header ("PULL 111.8 deg to the segment (+21.8 from perpendicular); moment arm 10.2 cm") and in the saved overlay video. Values are unsmoothed.
- **Blank, not guessed,** whenever the strap, anchor or a needed segment dot is lost or switched off.
- **cm values** need the ruler scale and use the scale as marked; they are only right at the ruler's distance from the camera. Without a scale the header shows pixels ("no scale").
- **Limit:** this is the 2D picture. A pull toward or away from the camera, or a rope that is not in the leg's plane, is invisible, and the real pull angle then differs.
- **Saved:** the six CSV columns (only when both dots are used) and `meta.json` `pull` (segment, joint dot, definitions; null otherwise).
- **Code:** `limbtrack/pull.py` `compute_pull`; `engine.py` `_compute_pull`, `pull_segment`.

## F. Calibration

**Pixel scale (new):** a ruler of 10 to 20 cm with a dot sticker centred on each end, placed at the same distance from the camera as the joint and square to it. In the "Scale (ruler)" box of the Live tab you type the length between the dot centres, press *Mark the two ruler dots* and click both. The app stores pixels per centimetre. The two ruler dots are **fixed points** (a click snaps to the dot's centre once; they are not tracked afterwards, so a shadow or hand cannot lose them). The app therefore does NOT notice a camera or ruler that moves after marking: mark the ruler again. It shows the ruler (blue rings and a line) in the live picture, in the saved overlay video and in Review.
- **Alters numbers: none.** It does not touch any angle or dot position; it only provides the conversion from pixels to centimetres for later features (pull angle, moment arm, displacement in cm).
- **Warnings:** ruler shorter than 60 px in the picture (coarse scale). (Until 2026-10-09 the ruler dots were tracked and a lost dot or a 3 % change was flagged; that was dropped because shadows and hands lost the dots.)
- **Saved:** CSV column `scale_px_per_cm` (the same fixed value in every row; blank if no ruler was marked) and `meta.json` `scale` (ruler length, pixels per cm at marking, the two fixed dot positions, `ruler_dots_are_fixed_points`, and the validity note). Review draws the ruler from these positions and shows `ruler 15 cm = 5.80 px/cm` in its title line. `scale` is null when no ruler was marked.
- **Limits:** correct only for things at the ruler's distance from the camera; an object 5 cm nearer or farther at 2.5 m is off by about 2 %. Lens distortion is not corrected, so keep the ruler near the joint and near the middle of the picture. The accuracy also depends on how exactly the dot centres sit on the ruler marks (1 mm off on a 15 cm ruler is 0.7 %).
- It cannot be changed while recording, and it is cleared when a new source is opened, the mirror is switched, or the dot colour setting is changed.
- **Code:** `limbtrack/scale.py` `ScaleReference`; `engine.py` `mark_scale_dot`, `clear_scale`; `overlay.py` `_draw_scale`; `gui.py` scale box.

**Dot-size check (new):** with a scale set, the scale box lists the approximate width of the smallest dot in cm and says **TOO SMALL** for any dot under the protocol minimum of 2.0 cm. The width comes from the dot's blob size when it was marked, divided by the scale. Until 0.7.1 it also subtracted 1.3 px for "soft edges"; that allowance was measured on test dots that OpenCV draws about 1 px wider than their stated size, and on discs of exactly known size the blob reads +0.3 px (sharp) to -0.7 px (soft picture) from the truth, so nothing is subtracted since 0.7.2 (`EDGE_BLUR_PX = 0`). A disc seen side-on (ASIS/PSIS from the side) reads narrower than its true width. It is an estimate: check it once against a disc of known size under your real lighting and focus, and draw tape-dot discs a little over 2 cm (about 2.2 cm, `MARKERS.md`). Saved in `meta.json` as `dot_size_check` (limit, each dot's estimate, which are below). No effect on tracking or angles. Code: `engine.py` `dot_sizes_cm`, `small_dots`; `scale.py` `estimate_diameter_cm`.

**Still not implemented:** lens correction, camera-tilt correction.

Things that look like calibration but are not: the dot size and contrast learned when you click a dot (A1), the dot-colour auto-detect (B), the "participant faces" setting (A8), and the camera lock below.

### A16. Force from the load cell (new, 2026-10-09; `limbtrack/force.py`)
- **What:** a load cell (S-type, with an amplifier) on one analog input of an NI DAQ (written for the USB-6003, 16 bit, 100 kS/s shared, +-10 V). The DAQ is sampled continuously at 1000 samples per second on its own thread; the newest 10 minutes are kept in memory. During a recording every sample is written to `<trial>_force.csv` as it arrives.
- **Alters numbers:** none of the angle numbers. It adds force numbers: volts are always saved; newtons = (volts - zero) x newtons per volt. Newtons per volt comes from the active load cell regression (`limbtrack/load_cell_calibrations.json`; the first is 526.95 from 24 runs with 0 to 250 kg, 13 Oct 2026: slope known to 0.04 %, single-run scatter 0.15 kg, a reading good to about +-3 N at 95 %). The zero is taken with the *Zero* button / Z key (1 s of signal, no pull on the strap), as often as needed, and is NOT kept when the program closes; until it exists newtons stay blank. New regressions are added with `LoadCellCalibration/logs_to_points.py` and `add_calibration.py` and keep their points; each trial records the id of the regression it used and Review's *Calibration* tab shows it with the precision of the trial's peak and plateau (precision = sqrt(scatter^2 + (load x slope error)^2); it does not cover the zero's noise or a pull that is not along the cell's axis). A known mass can replace the slope for one session (newtons per volt = mass x 9.80665 / (load volts - zero volts)). A zero or a known-mass reading is refused if the signal drifts (the first and second half of the reading differ by more than 10 mV) or is too noisy (spread with the 50 Hz hum taken out above 30 mV; until 0.10.0 the limit was 20 mV on the whole spread, hum included, which refused a zero that the hum could not have made less accurate: a whole-cycle average cancels it exactly). A known-mass reading is also refused if the signal moves less than 5 mV from the zero. The zero's noise, hum and uncertainty (noise / sqrt(samples)) are saved in the trial and written after pressing Zero. Neither can change during a recording.
- **Time alignment:** pictures and force samples both use `time.perf_counter()`. A DAQ chunk reaches the program a little after its last sample was taken, so the time of sample 0 is estimated as the smallest (arrival time - samples/rate) seen over the last 20 s; the error left is the smallest USB/driver delay, a few milliseconds, which is small against a 33 ms picture interval. The clock of the DAQ itself and the PC clock can differ by tens of ppm (a few milliseconds over a minute); the estimate follows that drift. The MVIC start marker gives an independent check.
- **Two channels:** the **voltage** is the primary record and is always saved, exactly as the DAQ measured it (input range +-10 V; a sample within 1 % of the edge is counted as `samples_at_range_limit` and flagged in the picture, because the peak may be cut off there). **Newtons** are computed from the voltage with a calibration and are blank until one exists. A calibration made later can be applied to old recordings in Review (*Apply the saved force calibration*): it computes newtons from the saved volts and never changes the volts; the export records which calibration was used.
- **Per picture:** `force_v` and `force_n` in `data.csv` = means of the samples between the previous and this picture (newtons blank if uncalibrated; both blank if no sample). The complete signal is in `force.csv`; use it for the peak (the peak of a contraction is short and lies between pictures).
- **How force and pictures are tied, and where that can go wrong:** the force is NOT sampled per picture. It runs on its own clock at 1000 samples per second and only the time stamps tie it to the pictures, so a slow or uneven frame rate does not lose force. The weak points are all in the time stamps of the PICTURES: (1) a picture is stamped when the program receives it, not when the camera took it. The phone, its video encoding, the USB or Wi-Fi link, DroidCam and Windows add a fixed delay, typically 0.1 to 0.3 s, and unknown until measured (tap test in `SETUP_AND_VALIDATION.md`). It shifts which picture belongs to the peak force by that delay. Review has a *Picture delay* box for it (default 0; saved in the export). (2) Frames arrive in bursts (the board recordings had 24 to 77 ms between pictures at a nominal 33 ms), which is +-15 to 30 ms of jitter in each stamp; the angle at the peak force therefore uses all pictures within 0.1 s of the (delayed) peak. (3) At 17 to 26 fps there are only 2 to 3 pictures in that 0.2 s window; in a steady hold the angle changes little, so this matters for fast movement, not for an isometric hold. (4) A dropped or frozen picture stream is flagged as gaps in `meta.json`; the force is unaffected. (5) The force clock estimate (see above) is good to a few milliseconds.
- **Smoothing and mains hum (new in 0.6.0):** the first real recordings had 50 Hz mains hum of about 90 mV on a signal of about 1 V (9 to 14 % of the peak above rest), and the highest single raw sample was 8 to 11 % above the peak of the same signal smoothed over 100 ms. Single noisy samples therefore overstate a peak. The program shows and analyses a **centred moving average** (default 100 ms): the live read-out, the live trace, Review's curve and every Review force number (peak, plateau, rest, angle at the peak) use it, and the raw single-sample maximum is listed next to it. At 1000 samples per second a window that is a multiple of 20 ms (20, 40, 100, 200 ms) removes 50 Hz and its harmonics exactly. **The saved files (`force.csv`, the volts in `data.csv`) always hold the raw samples**; the smoothing length is saved in the export (`force_smoothing_ms`). Review measures the 50 Hz amplitude from the raw voltage and warns above 3 % of the peak above rest (check shielding and grounding).
- **Window from the force (new):** *Window = force above half of peak* sets the analysis window to the stretch around the peak where the smoothed force is above half of the way from rest to peak. In the first two recordings the MVIC markers were pressed 4 to 5 s before the force really rose (the hold was 8.6 to 14.2 s and 13.0 to 15.5 s), so a window from the markers includes seconds of low force and gives a plateau that is too low (0.75 V instead of 0.96 V in the first trial).
- **Not recorded** with a video file as the source (its clock does not match the DAQ).
- **Review:** the force on a right-hand axis; for the window: peak and when, plateau mean (middle 60 %), rest level (1 s before), peak above rest, and the main joint angle at the peak force. In `_summary.csv`: `force_unit, force_peak, force_t_peak_s, force_plateau_mean, force_rest_mean, main_angle_at_peak_force_deg`.
- **Limits:** the sensor's own accuracy and linearity are not checked by the program; a load applied off the sensor axis, or the strap pulling at an angle, changes what the sensor reads (the pull angle is recorded separately when the strap and anchor dots are used); the amplifier must give a voltage within +-10 V (a current output, 4-20 mA, needs a shunt resistor, the USB-6003 has no current input).
- **Code:** `force.py`; `engine.py` `_take_force`, `_force_header`, `_force_meta`; `recorder.py` `write_force`; `review.py` `force_summary`; `gui.py` Force box.

### A17. What Review reads out of a trial (new in 0.7.0; `limbtrack/analysis.py`, `review.py`)
All of this only reads a recorded trial; nothing is written into the recording. The window is the blue band (drag its edges, or *Window = MVIC start to end*, or *Window = force above half of peak*).
- **Layout:** the video and controls on the left, the numbers in four tabs on the right (*Check*, *Angles*, *Force*, *Dots and pull*), the angle plot full width below and, for a trial with force, a force strip under it on the same time axis (raw samples faint, the smoothed curve bold, the window mirrored).
- **Check tab:** a plain go / no-go list for the trial, each line OK, CHECK or info: pictures per second and whether the computer was the limit; gaps; dots lost in more than 10 % of the pictures (the strap dot gets its own advice); dots under 2 cm; the joint angle during the hold against the target and tolerance, and drift of more than 2 deg between the start and the end of the hold; force calibrated or in volts; 50 Hz hum above 3 %; the signal touching the DAQ's range; DAQ stopped; the MVIC marker more than 1 s away from where the force really rose; anchor wander above 2 px; strap dot seen in less than 80 % of the pictures; no ruler so no torque.
- **Force tab (beyond peak and plateau):** onset (the force last below 10 % of its rise), the time from 10 % to 90 % of the rise, the steepest rise over 100 ms, how long the force stayed above 90 % of the peak and its steadiness (SD / mean in the middle 60 % of that stretch). All from the smoothed force.
- **Torque (N.m)** = smoothed force (N) x moment arm (m), per picture. Needs a calibrated force, a cm scale (ruler) and the anchor dot. The moment arm is the shortest distance from the joint to the line from the strap dot to the anchor dot; in a picture where the strap dot is not seen, the distal dot of the segment (the ankle: the strap sits next to it) stands in, and the number of such pictures is reported. The force is read at the time the scene was (picture time minus the *picture delay*). The sensor measures the force along the strap; the 2D picture cannot see a pull toward or away from the camera, and the moment arm is only right at the ruler's distance.
- **Export all trials of this participant:** one CSV row per trial in the participant's folder (`<ID>_session_summary.csv`): window used, picture rate, force peak / plateau / rest / onset / rise time / steadiness / hum share, the main joint angle at the peak force and the plateau angle, rest-to-hold angle shift, torque numbers, the CHECK lines, and, for trials of the same test and position, how much their peak force differs (`repeatability_pct_same_position`) and `third_trial_needed` when two trials differ by more than 10 %. The force numbers use the smoothing set in Review (default 100 ms).
- **Angle versus force:** in the two first force trials the knee angle followed the force closely (correlation 0.93 and 0.97) and fell 2 to 7 deg while the force rose (slack and stretch in the strap and the limb). This is not used to estimate the picture delay: the best-fit lag was broad (flat over 0.1 to 0.3 s) and not the same in the two trials, so use the tap test.

### A18. Automatic MVIC start and end markers (new in 0.8.0; `limbtrack/automvic.py`)
- **What:** tick *Mark the MVIC automatically from the force* in the Markers and sound section (needs the force connected) and press Record. The program watches the force; when the ramp begins it writes the **MVIC start** marker, and the **MVIC end** marker *MVIC length* (default 7 s) after it. The protocol's contraction is 1-5-1: ramp 1 s, hold 5 s, relax 1 s, 7 s in all. The markers are the same as the S / E keys' (same labels, `event` column, `meta.json` events), so the Review window, the Check tab and the exports are unchanged. Pressing S or E by hand takes the markers over for the rest of that recording. One MVIC per recording.
- **Alters numbers:** none. Force (volts, newtons), dot positions, angles and flags are exactly as before. Only the marker list can differ: a marker the operator did not press. The start marker is on the picture at which the start was called, about 0.15 to 0.2 s after the ramp really began (measured in real-time runs with the simulated force; 0.11 s for a pure detector on a 400 N/s ramp); the end is on the first picture 7.0 s after it. The program's estimate of the ramp's true start is saved (`force_onset_estimate_t_s`). Pictures are stamped when the program receives them, so the *picture delay* applies to these markers as to everything else.
- **The start is called when** (force averaged over 20 ms; rest level = median of the force from 3.0 s to 0.5 s before now, taken from the seconds before Record too): the force is at least *Start when force rises by* (default 10 N; newtons without the zero, since only the rise counts; 0.02 V when there is no load cell slope) away from the rest level, **and** has moved by at least 40 % of that within the last 0.25 s, **and** both stay true for 70 ms. So a slow pre-tension (8 N per second), a knock of up to 50 ms and 50 Hz hum are not a ramp; a rest that sways raises the threshold to 6 x its noise. Either direction of force counts. The constants are at the top of `automvic.py` and the setup and result go into `meta.json` (`auto_mvic_setup`, `auto_mvic`).
- **Shown:** the Record box and the picture header (and so the overlay video) say *AUTO MVIC: armed, waiting for the ramp* / *3.2 / 7 s* / *start and end marked*, or that a marker pressed by hand took over; the marker banner says *(automatic)*; the marker counts include the automatic ones. If the force never rises by the threshold nothing is marked, the box keeps saying *armed*, and Review's Check tab says so.
- **Limits:** tried only with the simulated force source and made-up traces (`tests/test_automvic.py`, `tests/test_automvic_engine.py`), not with the real load cell. The threshold must be above the real noise and below the ramp (a first session should show both in Review). Press Record while the person is at rest: a push that is already under way when Record is pressed is not reliably found (it may be marked at the very first picture, or its early part count as 'rest'). The end is a set time after the start, not detected: a contraction that is shorter or longer than 7 s still gets the end 7 s after the start (Review's *Window = force above half of peak* does not depend on the markers).
- **Code:** `automvic.py` `AutoMvic`; `engine.py` `_arm_auto_mvic`, `_auto_mvic_step`, `auto_mvic_status`, `add_event`; `gui.py` Markers and sound section; `analysis.py` `trial_checks`.

### A19. Pause for a break, with a ghost (new in 0.10.0; `engine.py` `pause` / `resume`, `tracker.py` `hold`, `overlay.py`)
- **What:** for a rest between trials with a camera running. *Pause for a break* (button or **P**) stops tracking and shows a pale dashed ghost of every marked dot (a ring big enough for the sticker, the dot's name, the limb lines of the angles in use) at the positions they had when the break began. The picture header says PAUSED. *Resume* (the same button or P) freezes every dot where it was and takes it back **only** when a round, right-sized, contrasty dot appears inside its small fixed circle (2 x the dot width: the lost-dot rule). The ghost stays until every dot is tracked again.
- **Alters numbers:** none. A pause is refused while recording, and recording, Lock and marking are refused while paused, so no recorded number can come from a paused picture. The locked position and its references are kept. On a return to the same place the angles equal those from before the break within 0.1 deg (`tests/test_pause.py`); a participant who comes back elsewhere is simply not tracked (dots stay lost, angles lost, ghost up) until the stickers are on the circles or the dots are marked again. *Clear all dots and start again* ends a break.
- **Limits:** the ghost is a picture of where the dots were, not of the posture: reaching it with the stickers does not prove the joint angles are the same (look at the read-out after Resume). While paused nothing is written to the angle trace. A dot that was lost when the break began has no ghost ring. Tried on synthetic pictures only.
- **Code:** `Engine.pause`, `resume`, `cancel_pause`, `pause_ghost`, `_paused_frame`; `DotTracker.hold`, `TrackerSet.hold_all`; `overlay._draw_break_ghost`; `gui.py` `_toggle_break`. The older locked-pose ghost (A13) is still parked and unrelated.

### A20. Sound cues for an MVIC (new in 0.10.0; `limbtrack/cues.py`)
- **What:** beeps that follow the MVIC markers. **Record pressed:** one beep. **Ramp registered** (the MVIC start marker is written, by the automatic detection or the S key): two rising beeps. **One second later (the ramp should be over): one longer beep**, then a short **tick every second** while the force is held (*Tick during the hold every*, 0 = none; no tick within 0.25 s of the relax cue). **One second before the end** (MVIC length - 1 s): two falling beeps, relax. **The MVIC end marker:** one low beep. The 1-5-1 tempo (ramp 1 s, hold, relax 1 s) follows *MVIC length* (default 7 s). *Test the beeps* plays all of them once.
- **Alters numbers:** no measured number. **It can change what the participant does**, so `meta.json` has `audio_cues` (enabled, interval of the ticks, ramp, relax and MVIC lengths, the cue names); switching the beeps off or changing the interval is refused during a recording.
- **Timing:** the times after the ramp are counted from the detector's estimate of when the force began to rise (about 0.15 s before the marker) or, for a start marked by hand, from the key press. They are played by a timer thread with `winsound` (tones made in memory; each call is synchronous because Python refuses an asynchronous in-memory sound). A call to Windows' sound system returns about 0.1 s after its beep ends (about 0.15 s after a long silence, 0.3 s extra for the very first call, which is why a silent buffer wakes the device when a camera starts); cues are kept 0.15 s apart. The camera frame rate does not matter. **How much of that overhead comes before the sound is audible was not measured.** No cue is played during a break or after a recording ends.
- **Limits:** the **constant** is announced by the clock (1 s after the ramp registered), not detected from the force; the ticks are not tied to the force level; the relax beep is a cue, the end marker is still the program's own. If the participant's ramp is much slower than 1 s the 'hold' beep comes too early for them. Needs Windows sound; silent without it, with `LIMBTRACK_SILENT` set, and in the automatic tests. The real Windows call was shown to run without an error on the development computer; the sound itself was heard by nobody here. **A sound that cannot be played is reported**: the window says so after *Test the beeps* and after a recording, and `meta.json` has `audio_cue_problem`.
- **Code:** `cues.py` (`plan`, `CuePlayer`, the tones); `engine.py` `_cues_after_start`, `_cue_now`, `test_cues`; `gui.py` Markers and sound section.

### A21. The Live tab's panel and the remembered set-up (new in 0.11.0; `limbtrack/panels.py`, `limbtrack/session_memory.py`, `gui.py`)
- **What:** the panel is a strip (ten step chips since 0.12.0, see A22; six in 0.11.0: the one line of what to do now, and Lock, Record and Pause) above sections of which one is open at a time. The chips are computed from what exists: the camera is running (done), the position (always done: it shows the target), the dots (done when every chosen landmark is marked, `n/N` shown), the lock, the force (off when no source is chosen; to do when chosen but not connected or not zeroed; done when zeroed), the record (to do until the participant code is valid and the dots are marked). The first chip that is to do is the **next** one. The summaries on closed sections come from the same state. When a step is finished while its own section is open the panel opens the section of the next step; a section the operator opened is left alone.
- **Remembered (`settings.json` > `session`):** camera by name or stream address, test, target, tolerances, dot colour, faces, mirror, smoothing, landmarks switched off, trails, overlay, force source and wiring and display smoothing, whether the force was connected (a switch-off on purpose is remembered too; closing the program is not a switch-off), automatic MVIC threshold and length and whether it was on, sound cues, camera model (not the checklist ticks: they are facts about today's camera), ruler length, participant code (only if saved within 12 hours; since 0.12.0 it counts as the participant only after Enter in the box or Yes when recording), open section, divider, window shape. Saved 0.8 s after the last change and when the window closes; written atomically, with the other parts of the file (the recordings folder) kept; a damaged file or a value that no longer fits (a test or camera that is gone) is skipped, never an error at start.
- **Never remembered:** marked dots, lock, force zero, ruler marks, privacy crop and masks, notes. The reasons are in `session_memory.py`: they depend on today's picture or rig, and a stale privacy rectangle would fail silently.
- **Opening at start:** if there is a remembered session and the tick box is on, the program selects the remembered camera by NAME and starts it (a camera that is not in the list is reported, and nothing else is opened), reconnects the force if it was connected, and switches the automatic MVIC back on once the force is connected. Not done without a screen or with `LIMBTRACK_NO_AUTOSTART`.
- **Alters numbers:** none. The widgets are the same objects as before, only placed differently.
- **Limits:** tried with a video file and a made-up camera list only; not at other screen sizes or Windows text scaling; the chips and sections do not know about a three-point angle chosen as the main joint (they show the test's own target).
- **Code:** `panels.py` (`Section`, `Accordion`, `StepStrip`); `session_memory.py`; `gui.py` `update_steps`, `open_step`, `session_state`, `restore_session`, `attach_memory`, `_auto_start`.

### A22. Ten steps, redo one dot, try the markers without recording (new in 0.12.0; `gui.py`, `engine.py` `dot_status`, `rehearsal_start/step/stop`)
- **What:** (1) the step strip has ten chips in the order of a session (participant, camera, privacy, position, dots, ruler, lock, force, markers, record); privacy, ruler, force and markers are asked every session and are done only when set or when the operator ticks that they are not used today (the ticks are not remembered); the beeps count as tried only after a test that Windows played without an error. (2) The landmark list is in the Dots section with the dot colour above it; each landmark has a state (marked, LOST, not marked, not used) from `Engine.dot_status` and a Mark / Re-mark / Cancel button: the next click on the picture marks that dot only, and the wait lasts until the dot is found. (3) *Try it without recording* arms an `AutoMvic` (same threshold, length, rest-level rule and cue plan as in a recording) on the live force, plays the cues, and reports the start and the end it would have written.
- **Alters numbers:** none. The try-out never writes anything (no `TrialRecorder`, no file, no marker); a re-mark uses the same `mark_dot` as a click next to a dot, and does not touch the lock. Starting a recording ends a try-out.
- **Claims about today are asked again:** the camera set-up ticks (stabilisation off, zoom 1x, focus locked ...) are not remembered, because each trial's `meta.json` states them as facts and a phone app that was restarted has lost its focus lock (the closed section says *n/6 ticked*); *I looked: nothing identifies the participant* is cleared when a new source starts or the image is mirrored; a participant code that comes back from the last session is a question (chip *P07?*, a *Same participant: confirm* button, an amber hint, and a Yes/No when Record is pressed), not an answer.
- **Limits:** the try-out needs the force connected (a video file cannot be in step with the DAQ, and it is refused during a recording); it ends after 60 s without a push. The "not today" ticks and the beeps-tried flag exist only for the step list: they change no file. A re-mark while the position is locked keeps the old reference pose on purpose (drift stays visible); lock again to take a new one. Tested with a video-less stand-in camera, the simulated force and a fake sound device; measured in a real window at 1500 x 900 only.
- **Code:** `gui.py` `STEPS`, `update_steps`, `_instruction_text`, `update_dot_rows`, `arm_dot`, `on_click`, `toggle_rehearsal`; `engine.py` `dot_status`, `rehearsal_*`, `_cues_after_start(automatic, auto=None)`; `panels.py` `ElidedButton`. ### A25. Hip abduction and adduction with the camera in front, and computed points (new in 0.15.0; `geometry.py` `hip_centre_frontal`, `protocol.py`, `protocol.json`, `engine.py`, `recorder.py`, `overlay.py`, `review.py`, `gui.py`)
- **What:** two tests, `hip_abduction` and `hip_adduction` (`"view": "frontal"`, positions 0 and 20 deg, no neighbour requirement, no strap/anchor dots), which completes the 19 positions of Tabel 2. Three dots: `asis` (tested leg), `asis_opposite`, `patella` (centre). Two angles each: **`*_goniometer`** (Tabel 3: fulcrum the tested ASIS, one arm towards the other ASIS, the other to the patella centre; the unsigned interior angle minus 90 for abduction, 90 minus it for adduction) and **`*_hip_centre`** (the same, with the thigh line drawn from the computed hip centre). The goniometer angle is the main joint (judged against the target and the tolerance, drawn in green/red); the hip-centre angle is shown and saved but **never judged** (`"judged": false` in its angle definition, a new optional key, default true: no reference, no deviation, no green/red, blank `_ref`, `_dev`, `_ok` columns). An angle that is only shown never stops Lock and never makes `all_ok` 0 (0.17.1; before, the blank hip-centre angle without the ruler made Lock impossible and every row 'not ok'). The hip centre is a **computed point** (`derived_points` in `protocol.json`, kind `hip_centre_frontal`): never clicked, calculated in every picture from the two ASIS and the ruler scale, usable as the end of an arm, drawn as a cyan diamond, saved as `hip_centre_x`, `hip_centre_y` in `data.csv` (blank when an ASIS is lost or there is no ruler scale), and recorded with its definition in `meta.json` `derived_points`. The equation (`geometry.py`, with the citation): Harrington et al., J Biomech 2007;40:595, set 1, right hip, mm, origin at the midpoint of the two ASIS: sideways (towards the tested side) 0.33 x PW + 7.3, down 0.30 x PW + 10.9, PW = the distance between the two ASIS (picture pixels / ruler scale); "sideways" is the direction from the other ASIS to the tested ASIS and "down" is square to it on the picture's bottom side, so either leg, a mirrored picture and a rolled camera need no extra setting.
- **Operator:** a frontal test replaces *Participant faces* by *Tested leg* (used to check that the two ASIS are not swapped: with the right leg tested and the participant facing the camera, the tested ASIS must be on the left of the picture, reversed when the image is mirrored; a mismatch blocks Lock like the other label checks), an optional *ASIS to ASIS, tape* box (cross-check of the ruler scale: a warning above 8 %), and the *Against a wall: heel, buttocks, back touching* tick (hip flexion 0 and the straight knee are controlled that way and not measured; saved as `sagittal_posture`). The tape measure and the tick belong to the participant code they were entered for and are cleared when the code changes to someone else (0.17.1). The label checks take the three dots from the protocol (the hip centre's definition, else the main angle's arms), not from fixed names (0.17.1). The Participant-faces check is not run for these tests. Without the ruler the picture header says *HIP CENTRE needs the ruler scale* and the hip-centre angle is blank.
- **Saved in `meta.json`:** `view`, `tested_leg`, `tape_pelvic_width_mm`, `sagittal_posture` (`controlled_by`, `measured: false`, `operator_ticked`), `derived_points` (definitions, equation, status), `hip_centre_scale` (source ruler, px per cm, the picture's ASIS distance in mm, the tape value, the difference), `facing_not_used`, and in each angle's definition `needs` (the dots clicked for it).
- **Alters numbers:** none of the sagittal tests (the tracker, the angle maths and the existing columns are untouched; `golden_tracking.npz` is unchanged). New numbers for the new tests only.
- **Limits and not known:** the constants were typed from a secondary source and the original table was not read (consistent with a cadaver study's 14 % of the pelvic width medial to the ASIS: 90 mm at PW 250 mm against 89.8 mm); mean errors of this equation in one validation were about 5.9 mm (sideways) and 5.5 mm (down), and soft tissue over the ASIS adds to that; the hip-centre estimate scales with the picture's ASIS distance, so a pelvis turned about the vertical axis or a ruler not at the pelvis' distance shifts it (the tape cross-check exists for that); the frontal projection of the 'down' offset is shortened by the cosine of an anterior pelvic tilt (my estimate: 3 mm at 15 deg); **the two angles do not share a zero** (synthetic pelvis 250 mm, thigh 400 mm from the hip centre: the goniometer reads -4.1, 0.0, 4.1 and 12.4 deg where the thigh is 0, 5, 10 and 20 deg out about the hip centre, so the goniometer's 0 is about 5 deg and its 20 about 29 deg about the hip centre); the ASIS dot has never been tried on a person in a frontal picture; nothing is compared with a goniometer or inclinometer; no torque from the front (the strap and anchor dots are not offered in these tests).
- **Code / tests:** `tests/test_hip_centre.py` (known answer, mirror, roll, scale, degenerate input, the cadaver cross-check), `tests/test_frontal_engine.py` (values for both tests, either leg, a rolled picture, no ruler, a lost ASIS, swapped ASIS, mixed-up patella, the tape check, the recording and Review round trip, the Live tab), new cases in `tests/test_protocol_validation.py` and `tests/test_protocol_table2.py`; the pictures come from `synthetic.frontal_points`.

### A26. Reference readings from a goniometer or an inclinometer (new in 0.16.0; `limbtrack/reference.py`, `engine.py`, `gui.py`, `review.py`, `analysis.py`)
- **What:** numbers a person typed from a hand instrument, kept with each trial beside the app's own angle at the same moment, for validation. Never a measurement of this program (blank = not measured; a typed 0 is 0 deg). **Live:** section *Reference reading*: instrument (goniometer / digital inclinometer / other), what it is read to (0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 deg), a box per angle of the test (and per extra of the test, `reference_extras` in `protocol.json`: the typed hip flexion and knee of the frontal tests), up to 5 readings per box separated by a space (a comma is the decimal mark; they are averaged and all kept), and **Reading taken now (G)**, which keeps the app's unsmoothed angles averaged over the 0.2 s before (`engine._ref_last`, independent of the display smoothing). **Saved:** `meta.json` `reference_readings` (readings with their mean, instrument, resolution, `stamped_at`, `app_at_reading_deg`, `app_basis`, `entered_for` = test, target and participant, a note, `titles`) and `reference_extras`; null when nothing was typed. **Review:** tab *Reference* (the readings beside the app and the difference app - reference, with the source: *recorded with the trial* or *added later in Review*), the dialog *Add or change the reference readings...* (writes `<trial>_reference.json` next to the trial; an empty box for a reading saved with the trial is saved as "removed"; `meta.json` is never rewritten), and *Reference validation...* (all trials of the recordings folder: per angle n, average difference, SD, 95 % limits, mean and largest absolute difference, the proposed acceptance of `SETUP_AND_VALIDATION.md` 4A, an agreement plot, and `reference_validation.csv`). `session_summary.csv`: `reference_main_deg`, `reference_instrument`, `app_main_at_reading_deg`, `app_minus_reference_main_deg`, `reference_app_basis`.
- **The app's angle in the comparison:** the one kept at *Reading taken now*; if none was kept, the mean of the first second of the recording (the table says which). The hold of the contraction is not used: the goniometer is read at rest and the limb moves under load.
- **Safety:** readings are tied to a position (test and target) and a participant (0.17.1: the code in the window; readings typed before any code belong to whoever is recorded first, then to that participant only). They stay for the second trial of the same position and are **never saved with a trial of another position or participant** (`reference_readings.not_used`; the Live section warns in amber). The boxes always show the readings of the position the window is at, so an edit can never save an old position's reading for a new one (0.17.1). Record hands over what is typed in the boxes first (F5 works while a box has the focus), and a box that cannot be read stops the recording. A new reading at a new position replaces the old one. Nothing changes while recording. Two trials of one position share one reading and one stamp, so they count once in the agreement; saving in Review keeps a reading that was not changed as *recorded with the trial* (0.17.1), so the pair stays one.
- **Alters numbers:** none. The typed values never enter an angle.
- **Limits:** a handheld goniometer is read to about a degree and has an error of its own of a few degrees, so it cannot show agreement to +-1 deg (use a jig with a digital inclinometer for that claim); the agreement statistics need several pairs per angle and are only as good as the placement of the instrument; not tried with a real instrument on a person.
- **Code / tests:** `limbtrack/reference.py` (parsing, saved form, side file, comparison, agreement), `tests/test_reference.py`, `test_reference_review.py`, `test_reference_live.py`.

### `meta.json` contents

## H. Unfinished or unreliable

1. **Mostly synthetic evidence.** The only real footage so far is one practice video with undersized dots (and one earlier recording). Thresholds are not tuned for real skin, lighting, straps or glare. Re-test, on a video with your real stickers: the lost-dot circle (2.0 x diameter), the partly-covered-dot thresholds, the dot-size estimate (no edge allowance since 0.7.2), tape dots (a black disc on white tape: glare, creased tape, sweat; `MARKERS.md` section 3 lists what was tested, on synthetic pictures only), and white and black dots together on real clothing and skin.
2. **No validation against a reference yet.** See `SETUP_AND_VALIDATION.md` (jig and inclinometer check, sticker placement repeatability). Until it is done, the angles are positioning data, not validated measurements. Sticker placement (a few mm) is the largest error source.
3. **The "raw" video is not lossless and not crash-proof.** Frames are re-encoded with the `mp4v` codec into MP4 files, which can be unreadable after a hard crash or power cut (the CSV and `meta.json` are safe; Review then shows UNFINISHED). With the mirror on it is the flipped picture. An AVI container with a repair step is a possible later change.
4. **The main joint is judged against the typed target, not the locked pose.** There is no "change since lock" number for the main joint (parked). The Review hold numbers (plateau, rest-to-hold shift) cover the analysis.
5. **Screen and CSV can disagree.** The screen colours use the smoothed angle, the CSV flags use the unsmoothed one, so near the tolerance edge the two can differ for a frame or two. No hysteresis, so the colour can flicker at the edge.
6. **Timestamps are the PC clock when a frame is read**, not the camera's own clock, so they jitter by a few milliseconds. Gaps are measured and flagged; dropped frames inside the camera are not counted.
7. **2D only.** Out-of-plane rotation and camera tilt add error; a pull toward or away from the camera is invisible; the ruler scale is only valid at the ruler's distance; lens distortion is not corrected.
8. **False precision.** Angles display to 0.1 deg, but real accuracy is set by placement and set-up (item 2).
9. **Stepping a practice video backwards** keeps the tracker state, so it can mis-track after a jump. Files only; not recorded.
10. **Marker timing:** a marker attaches to the next processed frame (up to one frame late) plus the operator's reaction time.
11. **Little tried on real hardware:** one phone-as-webcam app has been tried (DroidCam Client with an Android phone on 2026-10-08: it opens through Media Foundation, 1280x720, about 30 fps measured, no warnings). Not tried: Samsung S24/S25/S26 specifically, iPhone, Iriun, Camo, network streams on a real phone, the camera check on a real camera. The rest is tested against stand-ins (a fake camera, a local stream server).
12. **A half-covered dot is flagged, not corrected**, and a dot only about a quarter covered is not flagged; epicondyle/fibular-head swaps are only caught when the knee is not strongly bent.
13. **Force is untried on the real load cell.** It was built and tested with a simulated source; the real DAQ was held by MATLAB when it was written. To check in a first session: wiring, the amplifier's output range, the noise level, the calibration against known weights, and the time alignment (press the MVIC start marker at the start of a push and compare with the force onset).
14. **Not built:** two cameras at once, lens calibration, numbers-only recording, torque in the frontal tests (see PARKED.md), non-Windows support. **Built but never tried on a person:** the frontal view for hip abduction/adduction (A25).
15. **Automatic MVIC markers are untried on the real load cell** (A18): check in the first real session that the start marker sits close to the force rise in Review, and that the threshold (default 10 N) is above the real rest noise and below the ramp.

Closed in the 2026-10 work (see CHANGELOG.md): silent video-thread errors, unverified camera format and frame rate, tracker thresholds and provenance missing from `meta.json`, no mirror toggle, trackers swapping onto fast neighbours, the Auto colour sticking after a failed click, a stray click re-marking a dot during recording, controls changeable during recording, the keyboard shortcuts dead while typing, empty and half-saved trials, camera dropouts ending the session, a wrong "Participant faces" setting, the mirror-image three-point target, swapped landmark labels, the saved reference depending on the smoothing switch, targets and tests not matching the protocol table, the exposure lock blacking out a webcam.
